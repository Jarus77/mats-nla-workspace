## Principles

1. **State 1-3 concrete claims, then build the deck to them.** Readers keep only a few sentences, so pick them deliberately. [NN]
2. **Promise up front.** Slide 2 says what the audience will know by the end, with the answer included. Do not make people wait for the payoff. [PW, JS]
3. **Open with context or a story, not a joke.** Start from something the audience already agrees on, then introduce the surprising result. [PW, JS]
4. **One message per slide, and the title is the claim.** Write "Layer 7 heads carry the signal", not "Results". Before drawing a figure, ask "what exactly do I want someone to take away?" [NN]
5. **Choose the figure format that makes the takeaway obvious.** Figures beat prose. Cut anything that does not serve the message. [NN, CO]
6. **Show one compelling, hard-to-deny experiment before several mediocre ones.** Use diverse lines of evidence and confirm key results with a second method. [NN]
7. **State limitations and epistemic status early.** Say what you did not show and how confident you are. Competent researchers trust work more when it names its limits. [NN]
8. **Cycle on the key message.** Repeat it as promise, evidence, recap. Roughly 20% of any audience is fogged out at any moment, so use a recurring "what we know so far" anchor slide. [PW]
9. **Be precise and use consistent terms.** One name per concept, "accuracy" not "performance", and re-define symbols when they return. Keep colours and axes the same across slides. [JS, CO]
10. **Minimal text; the speaker carries the argument.** Slides support you and do not replace you. Be concise. [PW, JS]
11. **Treat explanation as research work.** If you cannot explain it cleanly, you may not understand it yet. Refine the idea itself (notation, abstractions), not just the polish. [CO]
12. **End with contributions and open questions, not "thank you".** Restate the 1-3 claims and what would change your mind. [NN, PW]

## Note on principle 12
The ending rule is our synthesis, with no direct quote. It follows from [NN] (few takeaways) and [PW] (cycling).
